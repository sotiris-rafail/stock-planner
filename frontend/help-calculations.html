How we calculate

Numbers on Budget and Progress come from your logged trades and these rules. They are illustrative, not tax or investment advice.

Budget plans

A plan has a start and end calendar month, an optional monthly amount, and optional per-stock monthly targets. Each month in the window gets the same monthly budget unless you leave the amount blank — then the budget is the sum of your stock rows.

Buys logged in Progress are matched to a month by purchase date. Spending counts toward the plan if the symbol is listed on the plan, or if “count other stocks” is enabled. Otherwise those buys are reported separately and do not reduce the monthly budget.

Set-aside is money from past months you did not invest (budget minus spent). A month is on track when every listed stock reached its target, or the full monthly budget was invested for plans without stock rows.

Currencies and FX

Each buy stores its trading currency. Budget totals use your plan currency. Amounts convert using the exchange rate on the trade date when available; if a historical rate is missing, today’s rate is used and you’ll see a warning.

Dividends use the payout date rate. Live portfolio value uses today’s rate. GBp (pence) quotes are treated as GBP.

Progress lots (FIFO)

Open lots are first-in-first-out. A sell reduces the oldest lots first and records realized gain using those lot prices.

Budget “invested so far” includes spending on buys inside the plan window even if you later sold those shares. Sells after the allocations feature keep historical spend via sale lot records; older sells before that upgrade may not restore full spent amounts.

Dividends

During the plan window, dividends count if you held the share on the pay date, the buy was on or before the pay date, and the lot had not been fully sold before payout. Net amounts apply your dividend withholding % from Account settings.

Live prices

Quotes come from Yahoo Finance or Stooq depending on symbol settings. Growth, projection, and market value depend on live data and can show “—” when a quote fails.

Past performance does not guarantee future results.
